00HOW

HOW I APPROACH LIFE AND SEE THE WORLD

I make sense of
what is complicated.

I approach life with curiosity, resourcefulness, and a bias toward action.

I believe most problems become easier once we understand the people, incentives, systems, and constraints behind them. My instinct is to ask questions, understand how things work, identify what is missing, and find a practical way forward.

I believe in institutions, hierarchy, discipline, and authority. Complex organizations cannot function without them.

Authority should make an organization stronger, not make its people smaller.

I have no problem with authority. What I oppose is bullying—the use of power to intimidate, diminish, or take advantage of someone with less power—and the people and systems that knowingly enable it.

The same principle applies to autonomy. I value the freedom to think and act independently, but autonomy without accountability is merely freedom from consequences.

I do not need to be the expert in every room. I would rather understand enough to ask the right questions, recognize who knows more than I do, bring the right people together, and create the conditions for them to succeed.

I am less interested in being right than in making things work. Sometimes I lead. Sometimes I follow. Sometimes I organize, negotiate, research, troubleshoot, connect, or simply make sure something gets finished.

The role is secondary.
What matters is leaving things better than I found them.

01WHY

Give me a problem
with no obvious answer.

I enjoy difficult problems because they create room to think independently.

I am drawn to challenges, neglected opportunities, and situations that require initiative. The satisfaction is not in making complexity look clever. It is in turning ambiguity into something workable.

Autonomy, to me, does not mean “leave me alone.” It means: give me a clear objective, the appropriate authority, and accountability for the result—then allow me to find the route.
